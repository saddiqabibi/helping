Our Work

Five Kinds of Practical Help

We keep our list short so that we can do each item properly. Which kind of help a family receives depends on what we find during the home visit, and often it is more than one.

Food  |  Education  |  Health  |  Water  |  Emergency relief

Food Support

Ration packs of staple items for households that cannot cover a month's groceries. A typical pack contains flour, rice, lentils, cooking oil, sugar and tea.

Who it is for

Families whose earner has lost work, is ill, or is on a daily wage that does not stretch to the end of the month, as well as widows and elderly people living alone.

How it works

After the home visit, volunteers buy the items and deliver them to the door. Where the need continues, we revisit before the next delivery rather than repeating it automatically.

School Fees and Supplies

Help for children who are about to leave school because their family cannot meet the cost of fees, books, uniform or stationery.

Who it is for

Children already enrolled whose place is at risk, and children who stopped attending recently and can be brought back.

How it works
  • Fees are paid to the school, not handed to the family.
  • Books, bags and uniforms are bought by volunteers.
  • We check attendance a few weeks later to see whether the child is still going.

Free Health Camps

A day on which volunteer doctors and nurses see local families without charge, give basic check-ups and hand out common medicines where available.

Who it is for

People who live far from a clinic or cannot afford a visit, including mothers with young children and older residents.

What a camp can and cannot do

A camp can spot common problems early and give simple treatment. It cannot replace a hospital. When someone needs further care, we tell them plainly and point them to where they can get it.

The next camp is listed on our events page.

Safe Drinking Water

A planned project to place a filtration unit where a lane or small cluster of households can use it.

Why it matters

Unsafe water causes repeated stomach infections, which keep children out of school and adults away from work. A filter at a shared point reaches more people than any one household could pay for.

How we plan it

We choose a site with the residents, agree who will look after the unit, and set aside money for filter changes, because a filter that is never maintained stops working.

Emergency Relief

A small reserve for families hit suddenly by fire, flooding, eviction or the loss of a breadwinner.

What it covers

Bedding, food, basic cooking items and help with a short-term place to stay.

What it does not cover

We are not able to rebuild homes or provide long-term housing. Where a family needs more than we can offer, we say so and help them find the right agency.

Being Honest

What We Do Not Do

No cash at the door

We provide goods and pay fees directly, so help goes where it was meant to go.

Not citywide

We work in a limited part of Lahore so that we can verify and follow up every case ourselves.

No guaranteed outcomes

We help families through a hard stretch. We cannot promise to end anyone's poverty.
