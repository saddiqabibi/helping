Who We Are

A Small Charity Working Street by Street in Lahore

  • Our Story
  • What We Do
  • What We Don't Do

Poor Foundation was set up by Lahore residents who wanted to help families near them in a more organised way. Many people give to beggars or relatives out of kindness, but there is rarely a way to check who needs help most or whether it made a difference. We wanted to build something local, accountable and personal.

We keep our work small on purpose. We would rather help a limited number of households properly than spread a little help very thinly.

We work in four areas. Monthly ration support for hungry households, school fees and school supplies for children at risk of dropping out, free health camps and medicine help, and safe drinking water for neighbourhoods that lack it.

Each family is assessed individually, and the help we give depends on what the home actually needs.

We do not give cash to strangers at the door, and we do not take on cases we cannot verify. We also do not promise results we cannot control, such as ending a family's poverty with a single donation.

We do not publish photos of people in distress to raise money. Dignity matters more than donations.

The Problem We Respond To

What Poverty Looks Like on a Lahore Street

In a big city, poverty is often hidden. It is the family renting a single room, the child who stops coming to school in the middle of the year, or the elderly woman who skips a medicine because the month is ending. People in these homes usually do not ask for help, and an organisation sitting in an office never hears about them.

Prices of flour, cooking oil, electricity and medicine keep rising, while the wages of daily workers such as drivers, domestic staff, labourers and street vendors do not move at the same pace. When work stops for a few days because of rain, illness or a holiday, there is often nothing saved to fall back on.

Because of this, we start with a conversation at the family's own door. It takes longer than posting a form online, but it is the only reliable way we have found to tell a real need from a guess.

How We Work

From a Request to Delivery

The same steps apply to every family, whether they contacted us, were referred by a neighbour or came to a health camp.

1. Home visit

Two volunteers visit, speak with the adults, and note household size, work, school status, health problems and debts. Visits are done in pairs so that no one decides alone.

2. Review

The notes are discussed by the team. We rank cases by urgency, such as a family with no food or a sick parent with no income, and decide what help fits.

3. Direct delivery

Volunteers buy groceries, books or medicines and hand them over. School fees are paid to the school, not to the family, so the money goes where it was meant to go.

4. Follow-up and record

We note what was given, how much it cost and when. A few weeks later we check back to see what has changed and whether more help is needed.

For Donors

How to Check Any Charity Before You Give

Whether you give to us or to someone else, these checks are worth making. We have tried to make them easy to apply to ourselves.

Can you reach a real person?

A genuine organisation has a phone number and an email address that are answered. Ours are on every page, and you are welcome to ring before you decide.

Is the registration status clear?

Ask any charity how it is registered and request the details. We will always tell you our current status honestly when asked.

Can you see what was done with money?

You should be able to ask what a specific donation paid for. We keep receipts and can answer that for any gift.

Do they respect the people they help?

Be cautious of organisations that show suffering to get donations. We ask families before sharing any photo or story and accept a refusal.

Is the payment channel in the charity's name?

Check the account title before sending money. Make sure it matches the organisation, and avoid sending to personal accounts you cannot verify.

Are they honest about limits?

A good charity says what it cannot do. We cover only some areas of Lahore and cannot take every case that reaches us.

Our Principles

Commitments We Hold Ourselves To

Respect

Help is delivered quietly, and no family is photographed without permission.

Fair selection

Cases are chosen by need, not by who knows a volunteer.

Written records

Donations, purchases and receipts are recorded.

Follow-through

We return to families instead of treating one delivery as the end.
